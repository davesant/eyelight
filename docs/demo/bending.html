Bending notes

Bending lowers the pitch of a note by changing the shape of your mouth. It gives you the notes the harmonica seems to be missing, and it's the sound of the blues.

How bending works

Each hole has two reeds: a blow reed and a draw reed. When you narrow the airflow in the right way, the two reeds interact and the note drops in pitch. You aren't forcing the reed; you're tuning the space in your mouth to a lower note.

Your first bend: hole 4 draw

Play a clean draw note on hole 4. Now slowly say "eee-ooo" while you breathe in, letting your tongue drop and move back. The pitch should slide down. If nothing happens, breathe more gently, not harder.

Draw bends on holes 1 to 6

Holes 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 bend on the draw. Hole 3 has the deepest bends: up to three semitones. Hole 2 draw bent a whole tone is the same as hole 3 blow, which is a useful way to check your pitch.

Blow bends on holes 7 to 10

High notes bend on the blow instead. Blow bends need a smaller mouth space and a lighter breath. They take patience, so don't worry if they come later than draw bends.

Overblows

An overblow raises a note instead of lowering it, filling the last gaps in the scale. It's an advanced technique, and it usually needs a well-set-up harmonica with low reed gaps.
