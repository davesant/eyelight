Tuning and care

A little care keeps a harmonica responsive and in tune for years.

Cleaning

Don't eat or drink sugary things before playing. Tap the harmonica against your palm, holes down, after each session to clear moisture. Plastic combs can be rinsed in warm water after removing the cover plates. Wooden combs should never be soaked.

Reed gapping

The gap between a reed and its slot controls how easily it responds. A reed that is gapped too high feels airy and slow; one that is too low chokes or sticks. Adjust gaps in tiny steps, pressing near the base of the reed rather than the tip.

Retuning a reed

Use an electronic tuner and support the reed with a thin shim. To lower the pitch, remove a tiny amount of metal near the base of the reed. To raise it, remove metal near the tip. Check the pitch after every stroke, because it's easy to go too far.

Flattening a reed

Scrape lightly across the reed near the rivet end, then pluck it and check the tuner. Work slowly and in good light.

Sharpening a reed

File gently near the free tip of the reed. Keep strokes light and even, so the reed stays flat.

Storage

Keep your harmonica in its case, away from heat and damp. Store it holes down so any moisture drains away from the reeds.
