Getting started

Everything you need for your first week with a ten-hole diatonic harmonica.

Choosing a harmonica

Buy a ten-hole diatonic harmonica in the key of C. Most lessons and songbooks assume C, and it sits in a comfortable middle range. A mid-priced instrument with a plastic comb is a good choice: it's airtight, easy to clean and not affected by moisture.

Holding it

Hold the harmonica in your left hand with the numbers on top and hole 1 on the left. Cup your right hand behind it to make a chamber. Opening and closing that chamber gives a wah-wah effect.

Playing single notes

The first real skill is playing one hole at a time. There are two common methods:

Puckering

Shape your lips as if whistling, and let the harmonica sit deep in your mouth rather than on the tips of your lips. Relax your jaw and breathe gently.

Tongue blocking

Cover four holes with your mouth and block the left three with your tongue, so air only passes through the right-hand hole. It feels strange at first, but it opens up octaves and chord effects later.

Breathing

Breathe from your diaphragm, not your cheeks. Harmonica is the only instrument where you play while breathing in, so you won't run out of breath if you balance blow and draw notes.
